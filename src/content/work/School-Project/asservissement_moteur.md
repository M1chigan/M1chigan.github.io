## Project Overview

This project, carried out during the 7th semester of the electrical engineering program (GE4) at INSA Strasbourg, primarily aims to design and implement a control system for a Direct Current Machine (DCM) . The goal is to develop a comprehensive control system regulating both the current via an inner loop and the speed via an outer loop .

### Test Bench Architecture

The physical system studied relies on the following hardware architecture:

- **Coupled machines:** The test bench consists of two mechanically coupled DCMs . The first operates in motor mode, while the second acts as a generator feeding into a 10 Ω load rheostat .
- **Power electronics:** The motor is powered by a four-quadrant chopper (H-bridge) operating at a 48 V input voltage, controlled by a PWM signal at a frequency of 10 kHz .
- **Sensors and safety:** The control system relies on a current sensor for the inner loop, along with a tachymeter dynamo (delivering 6 V per 1000 rpm) or an incremental encoder for speed measurement . A voltage limiter restricts the control signal between 0.2 V and 14.8 V to protect the chopper, which has a maximum voltage capacity of 15 V .

## Numerical Modeling and Simulation

To validate the theoretical approach before designing the control board, rigorous modeling was conducted in parallel on two software platforms :

- **PSIM:** Used for detailed simulation of the power electronics, allowing the observation of explicit chopper switching and current ripples (measured at around 18% for the motor) .
- **Matlab/Simulink:** Used for modeling the system using continuous block diagrams (where the chopper is modeled as an equivalent gain of $G_{h} = 6.4$) and for tuning the controllers using the PID Tuner tool .

Both models were progressively validated by comparing the responses of the standalone motor, then coupled with the generator, and finally incorporating losses such as friction and load torque . The curves generated by PSIM and Simulink overlap perfectly, proving the validity and equivalence of the models .

## Closed-Loop Control Strategy

The control system uses a cascade structure involving two precisely tuned Proportional-Integral (PI) controllers:

- **Current Loop (inner):** Designed to be 10 times faster than the PWM period (50 µs), with a target response time of 500 µs . The PI controller ($P = 3.5278$, $I = 19631$) achieved a simulated 5% response time of 565 µs and an overshoot of 17.55% .
- **Speed Loop (outer):** Sized to make the system 3 times faster than in an open loop, with a target response time of 110 ms . The dedicated PI controller ($P = 1.8838$, $I = 76.3682$) meets this goal by measuring a response time of 108 ms, with a controlled overshoot of 17.55% .

## Conclusion and Future Prospects

This stage of the project successfully validates the entire control chain numerically . The results confirm the robustness of the mathematical models and the effectiveness of the control loops . The next phase will involve turning this theoretical study into reality by designing the hardware control board (PCB routing) and conducting direct experimental tests on the test bench to compare theory with practice .
